We finished at 0.942. Here is what the competition taught us.

Twelve findings per knee MRI study, scored by macro ROC AUC, 4,407 training studies with free-text reports in nine languages and only 58 expert-labelled studies. Our selected entry scored 0.958 public and 0.942 private. Every entry in the field lost about 0.015 from public to private, so the ranking mostly held and the medal line came down to tie-breaks inside the 0.942 to 0.943 band.

1. Our campaign in one picture

  • Our own trained weights topped out at 0.914 (five-fold CoAtNet-224) and 0.950 (nartaa's S6 recipe retrained on our labels v2). Everything above 0.950 was public checkpoints we wrapped, qualified and reweighted.
  • The last 0.004 came from the public recipe, not from us. Per-finding blend weights gave +0.001, R6 with flip TTA gave +0.001. Our labels v2 tied the control exactly (0.950 vs 0.950).
  • Operational losses were large. Six hidden reruns failed with no score, one entry crashed to the constant fallback, one timed out at nine hours. Each cost a day of the five-per-day slot budget and a lot of verification work.

2. The shake-up was a shift, not a shuffle

  • Public rank was never a measurement. The public split is about 385 studies with a macro standard error near 0.003. The 0.958 block held 865 to 1,475 teams, and teams moved hundreds of ranks with no new submission.
  • Copies tie, and ties sort by time. Submitting a byte-identical recipe late puts you behind every earlier copy, on both boards. Our R6F v2 differed from the public vector only by weight variant, so we sat inside the block.
  • OAI-pretrained weights were worth +0.012 private. The same author's clean track without them scored 0.931 against 0.943. External pretraining on a related knee corpus was the single biggest lever anyone measured.

3. What the top teams did

Kaggle's writeup pages are not reachable from this environment. What follows is reconstructed from forum quotes recorded during the competition, the public 0.958 lineage, and community write-ups. The 1st-place post is titled "Correct Data is All You Need" and a 3rd-place post exists; paste them and I will extend this section.

  • Labels were the competition. Rank 2 (azamat1ch) on the forum: labels were made by AI, not by hand, and "don't rely on reports alone". Rank 39 (colum2131): pseudo-labels are generated from the images, with the image model taking the same input as at submission time. The winning title says the same thing.
  • The whole 0.956 to 0.958 public tier was one lineage: heliosli's ConvNeXt-small R6/R7 (288 px cache, full-field plus 100 mm knee-centred crop placed by a SKM-TEA V-Net, Effusion and Baker's stackers, six rounds of pseudo-labels) rank-blended 50/50 with nartaa's CoAtNet "Raptor" arms (384 and 224 px, OAI-assisted, anatomical mirroring, SWA epochs picked on the 58 gold studies). Flip TTA and per-finding Raptor shares were the last public gains.
  • Teams above the block had something unique: private ConvNeXt-S or EfficientNetV2-S ensembles at 0.2 weight, a five-family 47-checkpoint ensemble on their own LLM labels (0.951 public, no public weights), or single models reported at 0.960 with no recipe given.
  • Efficiency track was reachable. One single-model entry scored 0.948 public in about four minutes for 1,300 studies, once its inference input matched the training cache pixel for pixel (+0.013 over a mismatched input).

4. What worked and what did not

Worked

  • 2.5D attention MIL over slices and series with ImageNet-pretrained ConvNeXt or CoAtNet backbones. Three-slice RGB windows, 96 to 128 slice vectors, per-finding attention pooling. Every strong model in the field used this shape.
  • Anatomy-aware preprocessing: 140 mm square crop, intensity clipped at the 0.5 to 99.5 percentiles, right knees mirrored to left, a learned knee-centre crop as a second view.
  • Label quality measured against gold: an LLM prompt revised after reading the 58 expert reports (0.814 rule-based → 0.869 → 0.90 gold AUC), then a pseudo-label loop mixing LLM labels with held-out image predictions. Diversity in the final ensemble came from label lineages more than from architectures.
  • Rank averaging, fixed weights, one strong decorrelated extra member. The rule one author derived: a new member helps only if it scores at least 0.93 on gold and correlates at most 0.88 with what is already there.
  • Related-domain pretraining (OAI, fastMRI+, SKM-TEA) before competition fine-tuning.

Did not work

  • Frozen foundation features. Our frozen DINOv2 head scored 0.756; OrthoFoundation ViT-L reached 0.896 on gold and V-JEPA 0.80. Full fine-tuning of a mid-size CNN beat every frozen or giant backbone.
  • Bigger inputs and backbones on their own: 384 vs 320 px, Small vs Tiny, wider crops, native-resolution compartment crops all measured level. Longer training with EMA and heavy augmentation lost 0.003 public.
  • Tuning anything on the 58 gold studies or the public board. Gold-refit blend weights lost 0.001 to 0.003; seed noise on gold is about ±0.006. Our labels v2 beat v1 by +0.019 on gold and +0.000 on the board.
  • Weak members. A 0.86 model at weight 0.2 cost 0.007; our 0.9075-gold ConvNeXt lowered the public score. Adding a second compact model (S6) beside R6 cost 0.001.
  • Report-only labels for synovitis and lateral OA. The expert set contradicted the reports on these; image models cannot fix a wrong target.

5. What we got wrong

  • We built an auditing machine, not a model. Qualification gates, receipts, waivers and a 4,000-line handoff log consumed most of two agents' time. The models that mattered were trained by others in that time.
  • We trained for weeks on GPU budgets measured in hours. Our best own fit used 2,000 to 4,345 studies at 256 to 320 px; the public R6 ran six pseudo-label rounds on all studies with crops and stackers. We never reached the data scale where our label work could show.
  • We spent the gold set early. Ten label variants were selected on gold-58, after which nothing could be validated. The forum's own estimate of ±0.006 seed noise on 58 studies should have stopped us.
  • We chased the public block. P3/P4/P5 variants reweighted a 0.958 recipe by 0.001 steps inside 0.003 noise. The private board then moved every recipe by the same 0.015, so none of that work could have changed the rank.
  • Inference fragility cost scored slots. Hidden reruns failed where the visible three studies passed. The fix that finally held, salvaging every finished ledger before writing a constant file, should have been there on day one.

6. Research perspective: knee MRI detection

  • The field's recipe since MRNet (2018) still holds: per-slice 2D encoder, pooling across slices, per-plane models combined by a small head. The 2026 version swaps AlexNet for ConvNeXt/CoAtNet, max pooling for attention MIL (Ilse et al. 2018), and plane-wise logistic regression for a rank blend. No 3D network and no video or volumetric transformer placed.
  • Public knee datasets matter as pretraining, not as extra labels. OAI (osteoarthritis grading), fastMRI+ (pathology boxes) and SKM-TEA (segmentations) gave the two weights that dominated the board. Licensing was the recurring risk: OAI needs registration, and the host's rule was "registration plus licence acknowledgement is fine, institutional agreements are not".
  • Weak supervision from reports is a label-noise problem first. The winning pattern is LLM extraction calibrated against a small expert set, then self-training (noisy student) with image-derived pseudo-labels at the same input as inference. Report-to-image disagreement is finding-specific and should be measured per target before training.
  • Macro AUC over twelve targets rewards fixing the worst three. PF OA, lateral OA and synovitis sat 0.03 to 0.07 below the rest. A specialist head or a crop for the patellofemoral compartment is the open research gap; nobody measured a gain there.
  • Foundation models for MRI are not there yet. Frozen DINOv2, V-JEPA and an orthopaedic ViT-L all lost to a fine-tuned ConvNeXt-small. Domain-specific supervised pretraining at 2.5D still wins at 5,000-study scale.

7. Checklist for the next competition

  • Week 1: a reliable offline inference harness with per-stage deadlines and ledger salvage. Measure hidden-run time on the full test size, not three visible studies.
  • Week 1: estimate public-board noise (split size, macro SE) and never tune on differences smaller than two SE.
  • Hold the expert set for one final check only. Select on out-of-fold folds grouped by patient.
  • Find the public lineage early, reproduce it once, then spend all GPU on one strong, decorrelated member of our own (target: gold ≥ 0.93, correlation ≤ 0.88 with the lineage).
  • Prefer label work that changes targets per finding over model work that changes resolution or backbone.
  • Submit a unique prediction vector. Identical copies tie and sort by submission time.
  • Keep one experiment log and one state file. Every verification minute is a minute not spent training.
